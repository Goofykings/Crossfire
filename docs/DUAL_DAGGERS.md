# Dual daggers: models, animation and presentation (2026-11-06)

**Gameplay integration is now implemented.** See [MELEE_GAMEPLAY.md](MELEE_GAMEPLAY.md)
and the newest PROGRESS.md entry for controls, authority, cosmetics, tests and v20
deployment requirements. The presentation-only description below records the
original asset handoff; its statements about missing gameplay are historical.

Presentation only. This adds two original daggers, first-person arms, six animation clips
and the visual layer that plays them in first person, on remote players and bots, and in
the home pose lab. It contains **no** damage, hit detection, input binding, inventory,
crate integration or network synchronization. Those are for the combat implementation
(Codex). Protocol (v19), server and gameplay code are unchanged. Nothing was committed,
pushed or deployed.

## Assets

Generated by `blender/melee/generate_daggers.py`. All numbers live in the bpy-free
`blender/melee/dagger_design.py`.

```
blender -b --factory-startup --python-expr "import sys; sys.dont_write_bytecode=True" \
    -P blender/melee/generate_daggers.py [-- --no-render]
```

| File (`public/assets/models/shared/melee/`) | Contents |
|---|---|
| `dagger_r.glb` | Right dagger. 150 mm saber-ground blade, ring pommel, 3812 triangles |
| `dagger_l.glb` | Left dagger. 140 mm recurved blade, spine jimping, faceted spike pommel, 3440 triangles |
| `melee_arms.glb` | First-person arms: the player character's own arm, cuff, glove, finger and thumb parts on the unchanged 26-joint rig (1632 triangles) |
| `dagger_clips.glb` | Six clips on animated empties (no meshes) |
| `daggers.json` | Hold/attachment, spin pivot, first-person framing, per-clip timing, combo order, Blender checkpoints |
| `daggers-thumbnail.png` | Transparent 512 px render of the pair (for a future Locker/crate card) |

The editable source is `blender/source/daggers.blend`: models, arms, the clip rig and all
actions as `clip:node` actions in named NLA tracks. Model renders are in `docs/melee/`.

**Design.** The pair matches with individual variation:

- **Materials**, embedded PBR:
  - Two-tone blades: dark coated flats and spine, polished grind and edge bevels with a
    fine grinding-streak roughness map.
  - Gunmetal guard and pommel.
  - Graphite grip with a fine knurl normal map.
  - Anodized teal accent bands with a faint emission.
- **Dagger frame:**
  - The origin is the handle centre, where the glove wraps, 45 mm behind the guard.
  - The blade points along −Z and the edge along −Y.
- **Anchors** in each file: `grip_<s>`, `tip_<s>`, `edge_<s>`, `guard_<s>`, `pommel_<s>` and
  `spin_pivot_<s>`. Their positions are also listed in `daggers.json` under
  `models.<s>.anchors`.
- **Scale:** the daggers are true size relative to the character's hands, unlike the third
  person guns, which render at 0.85.

## How the animation works

Each clip animates empties in the **aim frame**: metres from the midpoint between the
shoulder joints, with +x right, +y up and −z along the look.

| Node | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `socket_r/l` | Grip socket: handle centre and dagger orientation |
| `spin_r/l` → `spin_back_r/l` | Finger spin about the flat normal through the thumb/index pinch point (`spin.pivot`) |
| `twirl_r/l` | Roll about the handle axis |
| `carry_r/l` | Reveal scale; below 1% the dagger is hidden |
| `pole_r/l` | Elbow bend hint, a direction from each shoulder |
| `body` | Torso rotation (yaw left, pitch up, roll) |

**Hands are authored hand-first.** A key gives the handle position, a blade-direction
hint, an elbow hint and a wrist cock (ulnar deviation, 20° by default). The design module
derives the knuckle direction from the same two-bone solve the runtime `ArmChain` uses,
so the wrist continues the forearm by construction. Measured wrist bend is 14–24° across
all clips.

The grip (`hold`) is a hammer grip:

- The blade exits the thumb side.
- The handle crosses the palm on a 20° diagonal.
- The palm centre sits 29 mm from the handle axis and 80 mm along the hand from the wrist.

At runtime each dagger is **parented to its hand bone** through the inverse grip. When an
arm cannot reach its target, the dagger stays in the fist. The browser test measures the
grip anchor against the enclosed handle centre at 0–1 mm.

**One rig system.** `CharacterWeapon` stays the only third-person arm/weapon pose system,
and its `ArmChain` IK solves both views. No second pose table or BoneIKController was
added.

### Clips (`daggers.json` → `clips`; seconds from the clip start)

| Clip | Duration | Timing |
|---|---:|---|
| `equip` | 0.50 | gun lowers 0–0.15, hidden at 0.15; daggers appear 0.15–0.22; right finger spin and left twirl 0.19–0.44 |
| `idle` | 2.40 (loop) | breathing guard, left hand staggered ahead |
| `attack_cross` | 0.62 | anticipation 0–0.10; **contact 0.13–0.22**; chainFrom 0.30; recovery 0.38; trail 0.10–0.30 |
| `attack_forward` | 0.52 | anticipation 0–0.09; **contact 0.14–0.23**; chainFrom 0.29; recovery 0.33 |
| `attack_finisher` | 0.82 | left finger spin 0.04–0.28; anticipation to 0.36; **contact 0.40–0.50**; chainFrom 0.60; recovery 0.62 |
| `unequip` | 0.40 | downward flip 0.03–0.20; daggers hidden at 0.22; gun rises and hands return 0.22–0.40 |

- **Combo order** is `attack_cross` → `attack_forward` → `attack_finisher` → back to cross
  (`next` and `stage`).
- **Guard pose:** every attack starts and ends in the guard. A chain crossfades 0.15 s
  from the follow-through straight into the next anticipation, so it never returns to idle.
- **Blend curve:** smootherstep, so a blend adds no kick. Tests show chains, returns and
  interruptions stay within 15% of the clips' own peak acceleration.
- **Cross stagger:** the left arm stays above and in front of the right while crossing and
  uncrossing. Forearm centrelines stay at least 6 cm apart, so the arms brush but never
  pass through each other.

## Runtime (`src/game/melee/`, `src/weapons/MeleeView.ts`)

- **`MeleeAnimation.ts`** (engine-free apart from Babylon's math types):
  - `MeleeClipSet` samples the extracted tracks.
  - `MeleeAnimator` is the per-character state machine (`hidden → equipping → ready ⇄
    attacking → unequipping → hidden`). It handles crossfades, combo chaining, a
    one-request buffer before `chainFrom` and frame-rate-independent advance.
  - `MeleePose` is the composed result: sockets, dagger-relative matrices, reveal scale,
    poles, body, and the `hands`, `gunLower`, `gunVisible` and `trail` weights.
- **`MeleeAssets.ts`:**
  - `MeleeLibrary` loads once per scene.
  - `DaggerPair` parents the daggers to the hand bones.
  - `CharacterMelee` pairs an animator with one character's daggers.
  - `holdMatrix` builds the inverse grip.
- **Third person** is a layer inside `CharacterWeapon`. Assign `weapon.melee =
  characterMelee`. While `pose.hands > 0`:
  - The hands blend from the gun grips to the sockets.
  - The gun lowers, muzzle down, and is hidden.
  - The torso takes the clip's body channel while the head keeps the look direction.
  - Clavicles protract and lift with reach.
  - Both index fingers wrap the handle.
  - Visibility stays in sync even off screen; the IK only runs when the character is
    visible, with the existing distance LOD.
- **Remote players and bots** (`RemotePlayers`): a `CharacterMelee` is created lazily per
  avatar and survives gun swaps. It is hidden at elimination (the death fade clones only
  character and gun materials) and disposed before the rig.
- **First person** (`MeleeView`): the arms GLB is mounted on the camera in the
  depth-isolated weapon group and excluded from the sun.
  - The whole shoulder rig sits `firstPerson.shoulders` from the eye, (0, −0.075, 0.02) m,
    so arm geometry and wrists equal third person.
  - It shares the gun's sway and bob (`WeaponMotion.pose`) and takes 60% of the torso
    channel.
  - `WeaponView.setMelee(lower, visible)` lowers and puts away the gun, and the muzzle
    flash is suppressed while hidden. The `Weapon` state machine, reloads and ADS are
    untouched.
- **Home pose lab:** `characterLab({ melee: { clip, time, play? } })`, test only. No UI
  entry was added; the API is ready for a Locker preview.

## Integration notes for Codex

1. **Local player:** the game owns `meleeView` (`Game.meleeView`). Bind a key and call
   `equip()`, `attack()` / `attack(stage)` and `unequip()`.
   - `attack()` returns the stage that started, or `null` if it was buffered or ignored.
   - Start authoritative hit windows from the clip's `contact`, not from animation events.
   - `reset()` is already called on spawn and respawn. Call it on death and round resets.
   - Block gun fire, reload and ADS while `meleeView.state !== 'hidden'`. The view only
     hides the gun; it does not stop the weapon state machine.
2. **Switching back:** the clip lowers and raises the gun itself (`gunUp`). Leave the gun's
   `Weapon` action idle, or skip its `equip` animation, to avoid a double drop.
3. **Remote players:** `remotePlayers.presentMelee(id, 'equip' | 'unequip' | 'attack' |
   'reset', stage?)` for events, and `syncMelee(id, clip, elapsed)` for late joiners or
   resync. Send clip name and start time; the animator crossfades on its own.
4. **Timing data:**
   - Read `daggers.json` (or import it in shared code) for `duration`, `contact`,
     `chainFrom`, `recovery` and `combo`.
   - Never put damage in clips.
   - Regenerate clips only through `dagger_design.py` and `generate_daggers.py`; the
     metadata and checkpoints are written together.
5. **Crate and inventory:** use the thumbnail. The daggers have no `CosmeticDefinition`
   entry yet.
6. **Trails:** `pose.trail` (0–1) and `DaggerPair.anchor('tip' | 'edge')` give a bounded
   blade-trail source. No trail is rendered yet.

## Fast attacks, flourish and limp death (2026-11-07)

This supersedes the clip table above for the attacks. Presentation and timing metadata only.

| Clip | Duration | Timing |
|---|---:|---|
| `attack_cross` (stage 1, right diagonal) | 0.25 | wind-up 0-0.055; strike 0.04-0.104; **contact 0.07-0.16**; chainFrom 0.17; recovery 0.15 |
| `attack_forward` (stage 2, left diagonal) | 0.25 | mirror of stage 1, same timing |
| `attack_finisher` (stage 3, crossover) | 0.40 | arms cross by 0.105, hold to 0.205 (~100 ms), release 0.205-0.248; **contact 0.215-0.305**; chainFrom 0.31; recovery 0.29 |
| `flourish_r`, `flourish_l` | 0.52 | idle finger spin, spin 0.10-0.34; the other hand holds the guard |

- Stages 1 and 2 begin in their own wind-up instead of the guard; `blendIn` is 0.07 s (0.09
  s for stage 3), so a chained attack blends from the previous follow-through.
- `shared/melee.ts` carries the same numbers in milliseconds (guarded by
  `tests/meleeCombat.test.ts`). Chain gates 170/170/310 ms make the combo cycle about
  650 ms (it was 1190 ms).
- Clips are exported at 120 Hz (`FPS` in `dagger_design.py`). `validate()` also checks the
  duration targets and keeps forearm centrelines at least 6 cm apart (the real-rig test
  measures the same).
- **Animator**: `play()` during a crossfade freezes the shown pose (`snapshot`) and blends
  from it. `daggers.json` has `flourish: { clips, interval: [6, 12] }`; the animator
  starts one only from `idle` with no blend running, resets the timer on any other
  clip, and `attack()` / `unequip()` / `reset()` cancel it immediately. `state` reports
  `ready` during a flourish, so gameplay cannot tell. `animator.rng`, `onFlourish` and
  `flourish(side)` exist for tests; `__crossfire.melee('flourish', 'r' | 'l')` for browsers.
- **Death** (`src/game/LimpBody.ts`): `CharacterInstance.captureDeath(seed)` freezes the
  current pose when the elimination arrives, then `poseDeath(elapsed, angle, axis)` runs
  seeded damped springs toward a limp pose (see PROGRESS.md). `__crossfire.eliminateRemote`
  plays the death presentation on a bot for tests.
- Tests: `tests/meleeAnimation.test.ts` (speed and pose targets, flourish, interruption),
  `tests/limpBody.test.ts`, `node tools/melee-anim-test.mjs`.

## Expressive arcs, blade trails and a heavier finisher (2026-11-07)

Presentation only: `shared/melee.ts` (damage 35, range, arc, contact windows, chain gates, combo
reset) and the combat protocol are untouched; only `dagger_design.py` poses, the clip trail
windows, `MeleeTrail.ts` and its hooks changed.

* **Attack 1 / 2** (`SLASH_R`, mirrored for the left): the daggers now cross almost the whole
  first-person view. Tip positions in normalized device space (90 degree horizontal FOV, 16:9):
  attack 1 starts at (0.77, 0.95) and ends the strike at (-0.76, -0.58), a 1.53 wide sweep
  (it was 0.68 -> -0.19, 0.87 wide); attack 2 is the mirror. The torso now turns *into* the cut
  (body yaw 0.30 rad, 0.6 of it shown in first person; it used to turn the other way) and the
  support hand lifts and tucks out of the blade's way, so the 6 cm forearm gap and the <= 23
  degree wrist bend still hold. Timings are unchanged (250 ms, strike in about 30 ms). Tips
  never come closer to the camera than 0.2 m (tested) and the crosshair is only crossed for
  about 20 ms.
* **Finisher** (`FINISH_R/L`, `CROSSED_*`, `OUT_*`): the hands pull back to the chest
  (z -0.27, right hand above the left), the forearms cross with bent elbows by 95 ms, hold
  until 205 ms (110 ms, within the 100-150 ms brief, with 1-2 cm of secondary hand drift and a
  moving torso channel), then both arms whip outward in about 40 ms to +/-0.31 m (grip NDC
  +/-0.94, blades nearly horizontal) and recover by 400 ms. The wind-up takes 95 ms against a
  40 ms release. Contact 215-305 ms and chain 310 ms are exactly the old combat metadata.
* **Trails** (`src/game/melee/MeleeTrail.ts`): a thin camera-facing strip along the dagger tip's
  animated path (tip plus 12% of the blade), smoothed with a Catmull-Rom curve, additive and
  vertex-alpha faded, soft edges, tapering tail. Attacks 1 and 2 use the striking hand only
  (cool white-cyan core 1.3 cm, wide halo 3.4 cm, 150/220 ms); the finisher draws both hands in
  a warm layered version (core 1.9 cm, halo 5.2 cm, 200/320 ms) with a longer follow-through.
  First person: `MeleeView.run` cuts an attacking frame into <= 4 ms slices (8 ms in Chromebook
  mode) and samples each from the socket pose, so a 30 ms strike is a smooth curve even at 30
  FPS; after the arm IK the newest sample is moved onto the blade's real position and the
  difference is carried into the next frame's samples (no seams). The strip lives under the
  camera rig in the depth-cleared weapon group. Third person: `RemotePlayers.trail` samples the
  real dagger tip and guard once per frame for visible players within 45 m and draws a simpler
  world-space strip (smoothed between frames). One shared additive material, two 56-sample ring
  meshes per owner (created at the first trail), disabled when idle; Chromebook mode uses one
  layer and no extra smoothing. Pure cosmetics: no gameplay state or aim is touched.
* Tests: `tests/meleeAnimation.test.ts` (screen coverage, depth, finisher hold/release),
  `tests/meleeTrail.test.ts`, `node tools/melee-trail-test.mjs` (real Chrome, 120 and 30 FPS steps, a bot, Chromebook mode) and
  `node tools/melee-anim-test.mjs`; screenshots go to the git-ignored `test-results/`.

## Limitations

- **Fingers:** only the index fingers articulate. The other fingers keep the modelled
  grip curl, so during the finger spin the handle briefly passes the closed fingers near
  the pinch point. At 360° in about 0.22 s this lasts only a few frames.
- **Reveal:** the daggers appear by scaling in from the hand (third person, and below the
  view in first person), not by drawing from a sheath.
- **Third-person gun:** it is hidden at the low point of its drop, not slung on the body.
- **First-person framing:** it was tuned and checked only at the default 90° FOV. The
  70–110° range was not checked; a narrow FOV pushes the wide anticipation poses toward the
  screen edges.
- **Remote poses:** they are only as accurate as the authoritative state Codex sends. The
  animator crossfades on its own.
- **Pose lab lighting:** blades read dark in the home lab, which has no environment map.
  In maps the polished bevels reflect the map's HDR.
- **Not verified:**
  - Multiplayer synchronization.
  - A low-end device.
  - In-game blade trails.
